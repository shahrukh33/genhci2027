About GenHCI 2027

Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) is becoming part of the everyday tools used by students and educators across Human-Computer Interaction. It can support ideation, prototyping, user-research preparation, qualitative analysis, critique, feedback, and other learning activities. At the same time, its use raises fundamental questions about how HCI should be taught, assessed, and practiced when AI can participate in activities that have traditionally been central to students' learning and design processes.

GenHCI 2027 provides a highly interactive forum for the HCI community to share empirical findings, teaching experiences, pedagogical approaches, assessment strategies, and lessons learned. Rather than simply promoting GenAI adoption, the workshop asks where, when, and how GenAI can meaningfully support HCI education—and where its use may undermine essential HCI competencies, critical thinking, reflective design practice, or meaningful engagement with users.

Questions We Want to Explore

How should GenAI change the way we teach HCI?
What should HCI students learn about designing with and for GenAI?
Which HCI competencies must remain distinctly human?
How should assessment evolve in GenAI-rich learning environments?
How can GenAI support learning without replacing reflection, critique, or engagement with users?
What evidence is needed to evaluate GenAI-enabled HCI pedagogy?

Call for Participation

GenHCI 2027 welcomes HCI educators, researchers, practitioners, and students interested in how Generative AI is changing the teaching and learning of Human-Computer Interaction. Participants may contribute empirical findings, teaching experiences, position papers, course or assignment designs, assessment approaches, case studies, or emerging challenges.

We anticipate inviting short position papers of approximately 2–4 pages. Accepted contributions will be shared with participants before the workshop and used to seed discussion and collaborative activities. The workshop itself will emphasize working sessions rather than conventional paper presentations, including experience mapping, pedagogical-pattern development, redesign of HCI learning activities and assessments, and development of a community research agenda.

GenHCI will be an in-person CHI 2027 workshop and will be open to CHI attendees, with priority given to authors of accepted workshop submissions in accordance with CHI 2027 workshop procedures.

Submission details and participant deadlines will be announced following workshop acceptance.
